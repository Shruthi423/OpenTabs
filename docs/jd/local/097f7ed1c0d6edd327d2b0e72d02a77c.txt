Cassidy is an AI automation platform that helps teams turn their company knowledge into AI-powered assistants, agents, and workflows. We’re building a product that allows non-technical teams to automate complex work without needing to become AI experts or engineers. Today, Cassidy is used across large organizations to automate workflows in sales, operations, insurance, support, marketing, and more. We’re growing quickly and are looking for a Product Designer to help define what the next generation of AI software should feel like.
The Role
We’re looking for an exceptional Product Designer who can own major parts of the Cassidy product from idea through launch.
This is not a role where you’ll be handed perfectly defined requirements and asked to make them look good. You’ll work closely with our founders, product, and engineering teams to understand difficult problems, determine the right product experience, prototype solutions, and ship them.
AI products introduce entirely new design challenges. Users need to understand what an agent is doing, configure complex workflows without feeling overwhelmed, work with probabilistic outputs, debug failures, manage context, and increasingly delegate entire processes to software.
We want someone excited to figure out what these interfaces should look like.
You’ll have a tremendous amount of ownership over the product and a direct impact on how thousands of people interact with AI at work.
What You’ll Do
Own the end-to-end design of major Cassidy product areas, from early concepts through polished production experiences.
Work directly with founders, engineers, customers, and the rest of the product team to identify problems and turn them into intuitive product experiences.
Design interfaces for AI agents, workflows, knowledge management, human-in-the-loop systems, and other emerging AI interaction patterns.
Take complicated functionality and make it feel simple and approachable for non-technical users.
Prototype quickly to explore ideas, communicate product direction, and test different approaches.
Spend time with customers and understand how they actually use Cassidy, where they get stuck, and what would make the product dramatically better.
Help establish and evolve Cassidy's design system as the product grows.
Sweat the details. Interaction design, information architecture, visual polish, empty states, onboarding, copy, and the small moments that make a product feel great all matter.
Ship frequently and continuously improve what you've built based on real usage.
