It’s built inside Ahrefs. That gives it fifteen years of web crawling infrastructure, petabytes of first-party index data, and no investors setting the timeline. We’ve been profitable and independent for fifteen years, so we answer to our users rather than to a board.
The role Software you operate yourself has settled conventions. A dashboard, a form, a table. Software that goes off and does the work for you doesn’t. What does it look like to hand over a task to something that will disappear for ten minutes, use twenty tools, make judgement calls along the way, and come back with a finished thing? How does someone see what it’s doing without reading a log? How do they trust it enough to leave it running, and stop it when they shouldn’t? What does a workspace look like when half the work in it wasn’t done by a person?
You’ll be the one working this out, and what you decide becomes the product.
This is a senior role that goes well beyond making screens. At Ahrefs we don’t have a conventional product manager layer, so our product designers take full ownership of their products and combine design, product management and leadership. On Letaido that ownership is wider than usual, because the surface is so new. You decide what ships, in what order, and what good looks like. Then you take it from the first sketch to the released thing and keep improving it.
This role gives you a load of autonomy, so the distance between your idea and a decision is one conversation. There’s no roadmap committee to win over and no design system to inherit. You’ll set the language the product speaks in.
You’ll also be one of Letaido’s heaviest users. When something is wrong, the fastest fix is usually walking over to the people building it.
What you’ll be doing Own the design of Letaido end to end: the agent experience, the workspace it works in, and everything a customer touches.
Shape the roadmap with the Head of Letaido, and with Marketing and Sales, who are heavy users of what we build.
Work out the patterns for agentic work. Delegation, progress, interruption, review, trust, and handing results back to a person who then has to stand behind them.
Research what people actually do with an AI teammate, not what they say they’d do. Go deep enough technically to know what’s possible and what isn’t.
Sketch and prototype quickly so bad ideas die cheaply, then design detailed, high-fidelity interfaces and drive them all the way to release.
Lead projects with engineers and data scientists, building, iterating and shipping, then owning the improvements and support that follow.
Set the design language and hold the platform to it as the team and the product grow.
